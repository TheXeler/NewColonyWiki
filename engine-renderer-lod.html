Terrain LOD rendering

Heightfield sampling

Far terrain uses a stitched heightfield rather than replacing each horizontal sample with a full cube. Each sample chooses the median solid surface height and the most frequent surface block, which prevents isolated decorations from changing the material of an entire coarse cell.

Sampling rules and stitching

Non-occluding models, transparent decorations, and plant blocks are excluded from the solid heightfield. Fluid surfaces are sampled independently and written to the transparent mesh. Adjacent cells emit vertical faces only from the higher surface down to the lower surface. Chunk edges sample the neighboring chunk's actual solid and fluid surface heights and stitch to those heights; fixed-depth skirts are not generated. Bottom faces are not generated.

LOD step and refinement

The requested LOD step still increases linearly between the full-detail load radius and the render radius, but terrain sampling is capped at four blocks. Thus the farthest 16 by 16 chunk retains at least a 4 by 4 surface grid instead of collapsing to one height and material. Approaching a chunk rebuilds it with progressively finer samples until the ordinary voxel mesh is restored.

Coordinates, culling and bounds

Planet display copies follow the same translation-only coordinate semantics as simulation wrapping; rendering does not independently mirror local vertices. Incremental mesh replacement refreshes the GPU culling height bounds instead of retaining bounds from the previous LOD mesh. Vulkan frustum extraction uses its zero-to-one near clipping plane, and CPU and GPU AABB intersection tests include the same conservative seam tolerance.

Mesh replacement

Transitions from a coarse chunk mesh to full-detail sub-chunk meshes always rebuild the complete resident sub-chunk set, ignoring any stale partial-dirty subset accumulated while the coarse mesh was active. Partial results prepared before a newer coarse mesh are discarded and rescheduled as complete rebuilds. The finished set is then published as one atomic renderer replacement, so the old LOD remains visible until every replacement mesh is ready. LOD surfaces consume the representative model face's UV range, UV rotation, tint index, directional-shading flag, and light emission. This applies uniformly to every registered block rather than special-casing terrain materials.
