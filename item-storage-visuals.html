Item storage visuals

Ground piles and owner contents

Ground-pile picking reads the visible RenderSnapshot.ground_items separately from ordinary entities. Rendering and picking share GroundItemVisual.hpp for the UUID-based offset, half-scale item model, element rotations and rescale compensation. Each model element supplies a pick box; flat sprites receive 0.01-block padding. The fallback uses the rendered half-block cube. A hit is compared with terrain, ordinary entities and zone planes, then stored as the pile coordinate plus pile_entity_uuid in the block-selection API. This selects the pile above its supporting terrain without needing EntityVisual, and routes the summary and management action to that pile before a zone or work point at the same coordinate.

A loose item pile is an ECS entity with GroundItemPile and Position. Its entity UUID identifies the pile. Partial pickup keeps the entity and remaining count; full pickup writes the backpack and removes the pile in a candidate ECS world before publication. Ground rendering reads these entities only and derives a stable offset from each entity UUID. The core:item_pile block is a render proxy and is never written into terrain.

Entity backpacks use ECS Inventory; block containers use OwnerInventory at the block coordinate. Each occupied slot stores a definition string ID, count, complete components, and any reservation quantities. Slot content has no UUID. Merging requires the same definition and every component, subject to both the definition stack limit and the owner slot capacity. Ground pile merging also preserves source work point, despawn timer, and recovery authorization. A full merge deletes the source entity, then resolves the surviving target component again before increasing its count.

Newly dropped piles automatically join an existing pile at the exact same position when their content and runtime metadata match and the entire new pile fits within the definition stack limit. The existing entity UUID survives, preserving task and reservation references. If the new pile cannot fit completely, it remains a separate entity with its original count.

The engine also checks existing ground piles on simulation ticks 10, 30, 50, and every 20 ticks thereafter. Piles at the exact same position can merge when their content and runtime metadata match. This periodic pass may move part of an unreserved source pile into another pile up to the definition stack limit; it does not consume a source pile with active reservations.

Numeric quality, spoilage, and attachment components must be finite. Owner insertion and item component JSON encoding reject nonfinite attachment floats or vectors before publishing an item. A nonempty payload_json must parse as JSON during content validity checks and save or restore, so malformed payload text is rejected before an owner slot or pile is published.

Initial supplies, crafting and mining outputs, refunds, and death drops create capacity-bounded ECS piles. Craft completion validates all products and publishes reserved owner material consumption, resulting piles, and removal of the CoreMod pending record together; a rejected source, product, or pending-state commit leaves inventory publication unchanged. Mine completion publishes its pile only after the mine depletion and pending record update is accepted. A failed mine completion listener leaves no pile; the next mine update clears the completed task's stale pending entry and can post new work. Task point output piles retain their existing numeric work point handle as runtime source metadata. Unloading a chunk parks block container contents with the owner block definition; reloading attaches them only when that definition still occupies the coordinate.

Reservations and hauling

OwnerItemRef locates a pile by entity UUID, an entity backpack by owner UUID and slot, or a block container by coordinate and slot. It also records expected content and a numeric reservation handle. Changed content or a reused slot cannot satisfy an old claim. Before an ECS owner is deleted, work cleanup cancels active work whose material reservation cites that owner and releases the claim, even when the owner is neither the work's worker nor its target. Death drop publication also rejects a backpack with an unreleased source or incoming claim. Disk and network restoration check references in both directions: every reservation must find its owner content, and every source handle in owner content must match a reservation.

CoreMod hauling scans recoverable ECS piles and rechecks recovery authorization when a work activates. It reserves the pile and target block capacity, carries the claimed quantity in a colonist's Inventory slot, then deposits into the target slot under the same handle. Failed delivery returns carried content to a new ground entity or retains the claim for retry while the carrier exists. If the source pile changes or disappears, hauling cancels the work and releases the claim; if the carrier is deleted, it cancels active work before clearing the pending delivery and claim. A carrier with temporarily unreadable position keeps its delivery for retry. The public Lua surface uses engine.tasks.reserve_owner_item; isolated pile-handle reserve and release methods are absent.

An incoming claim reserves capacity while the source and destination may continue aging. Its cached spoilage progress can differ from current content without losing the capacity claim. Actual insertion still compares every current component, including spoilage progress, before merging into an occupied slot.

Lua exposes items.owner_inventory, items.block_inventory, items.ground_pile, and items.ground_piles as detached content snapshots. Direct pickup, drop, and block transfer methods operate on owners and slots. A direct transfer rejects a handle currently managed by the work reservation system, so work pickup and delivery use the engine.tasks.* transaction methods.

Saving, spoilage, and equipment

Entity inventories and ground piles are saved with ECS entities and checked against the owner_items document; block containers and storage policies are also stored there. The network owner_items document must cover every authoritative inventory and ground pile entity. A pile without Position, an orphan source claim, a missing owner row, or an undefined field inside owner content rejects the candidate. Legacy item UUID or instance fields cannot be carried inside the new content format. The old item ledger documents are not read or sent.

Owner slot contents and ground piles age under their definitions. Block storage policy may slow or pause spoilage; a source reservation or incoming target claim defers transformation. Lifecycle notices identify a ground pile by entity UUID or an inventory by owner and slot. Equipment remains a complete ItemStack on the ECS Equipment component and nothing else. Exchange returns previous content to the source slot under ordinary component and capacity rules.

Storage snapshots match each block inventory's actual owner coordinates to its existing storage_voxel policy. They preserve the original policy UUID, location handle, filters, priorities and retain setting, including containers at rotated building points. An owner without a policy remains unassociated; the snapshot creates no policy or inventory count. Filter memoization distinguishes exact Lua safe-integer handles. Conflicting policies at one voxel reject the query.

Building-storage synchronization checks an existing usable point's policy coordinates, capacity and preservation settings before updating its managed reference. A mismatch refuses without overwriting preferences or moving inventory. Native single-policy writes and whole-state restoration also reject different active handles at one voxel; valid preference updates preserve the original UUID. These 2026-10-14 changes and their native/Lua scenarios have static checks only, without compilation or scenario execution. City runtime remains disabled and full rotated creation/restoration acceptance is still unfinished.

These source paths and their test cases have not been compiled or run in the current migration.

Collision, navigation and hauling

City death outcome preparation now resolves actual prototype death output tables using the native world-seed/death-Tick/UUID seed. Personal and generated piles use separate explicit identities and retain actual quantities, source definitions and exact seed in a blocked intermediate archive candidate. Native owner-death drops also retain the original Civilization owner when an equipment-only/ownerless personal stack or a death output has no worker ownership context. This keeps native and abstract city custody consistent; ordinary explicit inventory/worker ownership remains preferred. These source changes and native fixtures have not been compiled/executed, and final city memorial/terrain/log/notification publication is still pending.

The read-only drop capture requires an explicit maximum_piles preparation budget and counts real split stacks before allocating them. Candidate preparation uses the supplied pile UUID count as that budget. A large actual stack that would exceed available preparation capacity refuses while preserving both owners and output; this budget is not persisted as another inventory quantity.

City death preparation now has a detached C++ primitive in Save/CityResidentDeath.hpp. It reads original personal Inventory/Equipment and loaded definitions, retains every ItemStack component, and splits real contents under definition stack limits. Unreleased source/incoming claims, foreign custody and invalid definitions/components refuse. The caller supplies one explicit UUID per resulting pile; a private world receives those identities and removes the resident without merging existing piles. Failure retains the source and previous output. The enclosing city transaction still must authorize the pending death and settle Task/business references, output tables, terrain, memorials and notifications. This source is not yet integrated into archive publication or compiled/tested; city runtime remains disabled.

Rendered inventory piles do not insert collision voxels or navigation obstacles. Hauling targets resolve storage coordinates to the actual walkable feet height. Movement preserves a required vertical jump when horizontal displacement is zero. An exhausted route that has not reached its destination is replanned; unreachable work is interrupted and released. A route with no positional or waypoint progress for four simulation seconds also releases its worker and records a diagnostic instead of leaving the worker permanently busy.
